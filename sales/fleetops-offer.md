# KODYRA FleetOps — Commercial Offer v0.1

**Positioning:** a tailored operations system for independent car-rental agencies that need clearer reservations, vehicle assignment, and management reporting.

**Landing page:** https://kodyra-studio.elghoualizakariae.workers.dev/solutions/fleetops/  
**Working demonstration:** https://kodyra-fleetops-live.elghoualizakariae.workers.dev/

> Product maturity: The FleetOps demo is a functional, fictional-data prototype. It is not secure, multi-user production software, a real customer deployment, or evidence of paid commercial outcomes. Every production build must be scoped, implemented and tested separately.

## Ideal starting customer

An independent agency with multiple vehicles and an identifiable operational pain point such as booking coordination, unclear availability, inconsistent fleet records or time-consuming reporting. Start with agencies that can explain how they currently handle reservations and who would approve a software purchase. Do not assume all agencies have these problems.

## Value proposition

> Bring your reservations, vehicle status and basic rental reporting into a clearer workflow, tailored to how your agency operates.

Lead with a **business problem**, not a list of frameworks. Show the demo to illustrate one approach, then listen to the customer's workflow.

## Scope-based service structure

### A. Workflow discovery & proposal

- Map how bookings arrive and how vehicles are allocated.
- List staff roles, data sources and current pain points.
- Define an MVP and acceptance criteria.
- Deliver a short written recommendation, delivery options and estimate.
- Whether discovery is free or paid must be agreed explicitly. **Do not promise a free consultation by default.**

### B. Operational MVP (project-specific quote)

Possible scope: booking workflow, vehicle/customer records, a shared database, basic roles, and a straightforward dashboard. Define clear data handling, hosting, access rules, testing, handover, and what *will not* be delivered. Avoid promising features without a technical review.

### C. Production extension (subsequent quote)

Potential features only where justified: multi-location inventory, customer messaging, payments, deposits, maintenance scheduling, document management, integrations, observability, backups, and staff permissions. Each requires requirements, security and compliance review, integration testing, and operations planning.

**No public fixed-price promise.** Quote on deliverables, dependencies, acceptance tests, hosting responsibilities, maintenance, and staged payment terms. Do not invent a commercial track record, guarantees, savings percentages or customer testimonials.

## Qualification checklist

Ask one or two questions at a time during a live conversation:
1. How many vehicles and branches do you operate?
2. Where do booking requests come from (phone, WhatsApp, website, booking portals)?
3. How do employees see if a vehicle is already reserved?
4. What happens when two staff members handle the same vehicle?
5. Which reports would you like to have without manual calculations?
6. Who would use the system? Who can approve the project?
7. Which existing tools or spreadsheets must we migrate or connect?
8. What is the desired go-live window and budget range?

## Definition of a qualified opportunity

A named business with a real decision-maker or accountable contact; a clearly articulated workflow or customer experience problem; a plausible budget or willingness to discuss scope; and permission for follow-up. A website visit alone is not a lead.

## Delivery quality gate

Before any commercial contract: confirm skills and staffing capacity, validate a production architecture, produce a realistic statement of work, include security/privacy requirements, define testing and acceptance criteria, decide who operates hosting and backups, and avoid taking payment for outcomes we cannot responsibly deliver.
